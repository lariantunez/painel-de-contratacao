00 · The challenge

The hire nobody is watching.

A candidate goes through an interview, feedback, a pre-employment medical exam and the hire itself. Halfway through, though, the process leaves HR's hands: after approval, the store manager has to send the assignment, the new hire's work location. Until it arrives, nothing moves, and the start date keeps getting closer.

Who is stuck, for how many days, with which manager, and who has already been followed up with? The answer needs to be on the screen, not in someone's memory.

FUNNEL

Every candidate in their place

A board with one column per stage, counts in the funnel and HR's next action written on each card.

BOTTLENECK

Waiting for the assignment

The Awaiting assignment stage has its own traffic light, a configurable deadline and an upcoming-start alert.

FOLLOW-UP

From pending item to email

Pending items become ready-to-send emails to the manager, and each follow-up is logged with date and user.

01 · Principles

The bottleneck at the center, the history intact.

Four rules guided the dashboard's decisions:

01

Watch the stage that stalls

Stage 06, Awaiting assignment, is treated as the bottleneck in the code: it has its own color in the funnel, urgency sorting, filters, follow-ups and dedicated indicators.

02

Each stage asks for the data that defines it

Dragging to Interview scheduled asks for date and time; to Awaiting assignment, the expected start date; to Medical exam, the exam's date and time. Without that data, the candidate doesn't advance.

03

Nothing disappears without a record

Closing requires a reason and a detail, both saved to the history. Removing is separate, asks for confirmation and warns that it can't be undone. Any closed process can be reopened.

04

The next action always in sight

Each card says what HR needs to do now, and the Activities for today list brings together the day's interviews, exams, feedback and overdue assignments, by priority.

02 · The nine stages

From résumé received to hire completed.

The board has one column for each of the first eight stages; the ninth, Hire completed, goes to the history. Each card shows HR's next action:

Résumé received

send the résumé to the HR manager for approval

HR manager approval

wait for the HR manager's approval and schedule the interview

Interview scheduled

conduct the interview · date and time required on entry

Interview completed

record the interview feedback

Feedback given

request the start date to wait for the assignment to be released

Awaiting assignment

follow up with the manager for the assignment and track the reply · expected start date required

Assignment received

schedule the pre-employment medical exam

Medical exam scheduled

track the exam and complete the hire · date and time required

Hire completed

process completed, with date, time and the user who approved it

Drag and drop

Cards change stage by dragging and dropping between columns. In the bottleneck column, cards are sorted by the traffic light (critical, overdue, attention, on time); in the others, by opening date.

Close with a reason

Rejected by HR, failed the medical exam, candidate withdrew, position canceled by the store, or other. The detail is required, and the stage where the process stopped is saved.

03 · Assignment traffic light

Days stuck and an upcoming start, in a single color.

Every candidate in Awaiting assignment gets a band, calculated from the days since entering the stage and the expected start date entered when advancing:

BandRuleWhat the dashboard does
🟢 On time0 to 2 days waitingTracks it with no alert
⚠️ Attention3 to 4 days waitingAdded to Activities for today as “track assignment”
🔴 Overdue5 days or moreFollow up with manager button on the card and a priority activity
🚨 CriticalExpected start in 2 days or less, regardless of how long it has been waitingMoves to the top of the column and of the day's list

The critical rule comes first: a candidate stuck for one day but starting the day after tomorrow is already critical. Separate from the traffic light, the Follow up with manager after N days field (3 by default) sets when the funnel marks candidates as overdue and the card turns red.

An assignment only counts as received on time if it arrived before the candidate turned red or critical.

04 · Indicators and ranking

Seven numbers, each with its own math.

The overall summary has seven indicators. Clicking any of them opens the list of candidates behind the number and, for calculated ones, the math written out in full, for example: “3 assignment(s) received outside the red/critical bands ÷ 3 assignment(s) received = 100%”.

In progress

Neither closed nor hired.

Awaiting assignment

In stage 06, with time in the stage.

Attention

3 to 4 days waiting.

Critical

Upcoming start with no assignment.

Average time

Sum of completed waits ÷ assignments received.

% on time

Received before the red or critical band.

% late

Received already in the red or critical band.

Performance ranking by manager

Each manager gets a stacked bar with assignments on time, late, waiting up to 4 days and waiting more than 4 days, plus the average sending time. The default order uses a score that rewards timeliness and penalizes delays:

score = % on time + 8 × on time − 12 × late
          − 10 × waiting more than 4 days
          − min(average time in hours ÷ 6, 20)

Five other sort orders are one click away: most assignments on time, most late, lowest average time, most waiting up to 4 days and most waiting more than 4 days.

05 · Manager follow-ups

From pending item to ready email, with a record.

The follow-up panel groups the candidates awaiting an assignment by manager. A follow-up opens a pre-written email in the email client (Outlook), with the list of candidates, the store and the days waiting, and saves who followed up and when.

Three ways to follow up

Individually, from the overdue or critical card; consolidated, by manager; or all at once, grouped by email address.

Guided sending

In a batch, the dashboard opens one email at a time. When you come back, “mark as sent and open next” logs the follow-up and moves down the queue.

Latest follow-up for the current stage

The manager's row shows only follow-ups made after entering the current stage, so they aren't confused with older ones.

06 · Data and rules

An application carries its own history.

Each application is a single record with the current stage, the moment it entered it (entrouEm) and two lists: the stage history and the follow-ups. The indicators are calculated from these lists.

Rules that keep the numbers honest

ProblemMoving a candidate back a stage would leave passages in the history that no longer apply.
→
SolutionThe history is trimmed: when going back to a stage already visited, the later passages are removed; when advancing, only the new stage is appended.
ProblemOlder records used different stage names, such as “Feedback dado” or “Contratada”.
→
SolutionA mapping table translates the old names, and a migration adjusts the stage only once (flag etapasMigradasV2).
ProblemEach candidate's stage is stored as a number. Adding “HR manager approval” as stage 02, with the panel already in use, would have shown every candidate one column behind.
→
SolutionA second migration (etapasMigradasV3) moves stages from 02 onward up one position, only once per record. The server rejects saves from tabs still running the previous version and asks for a page reload, and the change was tested on a copy of the database before release.
ProblemNames, stores and managers typed in different ways would break grouping and filters.
→
SolutionCandidate name in uppercase, store and manager in title case, normalized spaces and accent-insensitive search.
ProblemTwo HR people editing the same card could overwrite each other.
→
SolutionA per-card edit lock, renewed every 8 seconds: anyone who opens a card in use sees “being edited by…”. In the demo, with no server, the lock list stays empty.
ProblemA YYYY-MM-DD date read as UTC can fall on the previous day in Brazil's time zone.
→
SolutionInterview, exam and expected start are read as local dates, and comparisons with today are made in calendar days.
07 · Screens and access

One work page and one user page.

The main screen brings together, from top to bottom: overall summary, quick filters, funnel, activities for today, follow-up panel, board by stage, ranking by manager and application history (closed without a hire and completed hires). Each candidate's name opens the full timeline, with the date, time and user of every step.

Access roles

ADMIN

Administrator

Accesses User management: creates, edits, activates, deactivates and removes users.

USER

HR user

Operates the dashboard. To open User management, they need to sign in with an administrator account.

PRIMARY

Primary administrator

Can't be removed, deactivated, demoted or renamed; appears as “Adm pai” (parent admin) in the list.

Registration asks for a login, an email for password resets, a role, a status and a password of at least 6 characters. The sign-in screen has “Forgot my password”, with a reset link sent to the registered email; in the demo, nothing is sent.
